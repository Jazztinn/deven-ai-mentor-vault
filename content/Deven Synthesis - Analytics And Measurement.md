## Principles

### Keep opening swipe behavior distinct from later viewing

Sources discuss staying past the opening, average view duration (AVD), rewatching, completion, and estimated retention. Treat them as separate measures: a first-seconds swipe signal does not describe later viewing, while AVD is a mean and cannot identify an exact drop timestamp. Some creators prioritize swipe ratio, others AVD or retention, and some say a perfect swipe ratio is unnecessary.

**When It Applies:** Metric names vary by platform and source; several captions use AVD and retention interchangeably or leave a formula unclear.

**Researcher Application:** For each video, record opening swipe behavior, AVD in seconds, video length, any derived AVD/length ratio, and completion separately. Use the actual retention curve plus the matching scene to investigate a specific drop.

**Limits:** Creator definitions and platform mechanisms remain attributed. Caption evidence does not provide actual analytics screens or videos.

### Retention ratio is a derived estimate with a denominator

Many speakers define estimated retention as AVD divided by video length. Examples include 30/30 = 100%, 32/34 ≈ 94%, 33/32 ≈ 103%, and 47/52 ≈ 90%. Other sources describe retention as completion or rewatching, so preserve source definition and arithmetic rather than silently normalizing terms.

**When It Applies:** Use same units and a clearly stated video length; reported values and captions may contain errors or ambiguities.

**Researcher Application:** If deriving a ratio, show AVD and video length alongside it, and label the result as AVD/length rather than platform completion unless dashboard definition confirms that meaning.

**Limits:** The corpus cannot confirm which derived ratios are officially exposed or used for ranking.

### Interpret benchmarks by metric, format and goal

The corpus offers swipe targets such as 75–85%, 80% and 81.1%+, alongside AVD goals in seconds and AVD/length goals that can exceed 100%. A target for a short replayable clip is a different claim from a target for long-form viewing; those differences alone are not contradictions. Some remaining benchmarks and priority claims differ under incompletely specified conditions. Reported examples below 100% qualify claims that overlength viewing is universally necessary, but do not establish a predictive cutoff.

**When It Applies:** Targets differ by creator, platform, niche, video length, audience and outcome. Some AVD goals are ambiguous between seconds and percent; reported cases may have unclear windows or metric labels.

**Researcher Application:** Treat one target as a creator hypothesis only when format, audience, denominator, time window, and desired outcome match; compare it with the channel’s own baseline.

**Limits:** No independent sample or causal design establishes a universal metric threshold or guarantees reach.

### Use analytics to form a specific, reversible test

Sources recommend examining per-video metrics, inspecting a retention curve and its scene, and considering pacing, captions, visuals, sound, openings, CTAs or endings. The mentor procedure is a researcher proposal: identify one observation, state one hypothesis and test one change while preserving a compatible comparison. A mean AVD cannot identify a drop timestamp. Existing-upload edits require separate consideration of current editor constraints.

**When It Applies:** Several sources propose different windows, including the first 24 hours, the past five days, and the past seven days; these answer different questions and are not interchangeable. Aggregate values can be affected by audience mix or private videos, per creator claims.

**Researcher Application:** Choose one video, one measure and a defined window. Prefer testing a change in the next draft or upload. If an existing-upload edit is requested, retain the original asset and pre-edit measurements, inspect the scene, and check the current editor limitations described in the external definitions note.

**Limits:** The corpus does not verify dashboard readings, whether proposed changes caused outcomes, or current feature availability.

### Average duration is not a moment in the video

A source suggests inspecting the video at its average-view-duration point. Treat this as a source heuristic, not a measured drop location. ## Decision Rules

### A Short has low views or reaches a plateau.

**Next Move:** Preserve a first-24-hour or other explicitly defined snapshot. Compare opening behavior, AVD seconds, normalized viewing, length, audience and traffic context against compatible uploads from the same channel. Choose one hypothesis for the next draft or upload, or collect missing data. A low view count alone does not identify a creative defect.

**Reason:** Creators propose swipe ratio, AVD, niche and audience as different possible factors, and some say strong metrics can still plateau. No single diagnostic is established. Do not use AVD as an exact drop timestamp.

**Basis:** researcher inference.
