This is a researcher-designed workflow grounded in the integrated source advice. It supplies a repeatable way to decide what to do next; the caption corpus does not validate a universal pass/fail score or causal formula.

## Reviewing The First 24 Hours

Confirm **the first 24 hours since publication**, the observation time and timezone, and the content version. A rolling “last 24 hours” channel total is a different window. If the video is younger than 24 hours, retain its actual age and compare only compatible observations.

Write the goal, format, duration and intended audience. Then record the available measures with their platform definitions: views, opening/stayed-to-watch measure, average view duration, average percentage viewed, retention curve, traffic source, subscriber changes and the actions relevant to the goal. Do not invent missing fields.

YouTube’s current Shorts documentation distinguishes staying past the initial seconds from later average viewing, with AVD/APV calculated among viewers who stayed. Engaged views exclude loops. Record the actual data definition rather than borrowing an older source’s denominator. [YouTube content-performance definitions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en).

Choose comparable prior videos from the same channel where possible. Match age/window, format, length, topic/audience and content version. Keep the baseline records visible. An ineligible comparison or lack of a baseline supports a limited reading, not a confident channel verdict. The corpus provides no universal minimum sample size.

## Choose The Next Move

| Observation | What It Can Suggest | Next Move |
|---|---|---|
| Opening response is weak against a usable baseline | First frame, premise, first line, audience match or exposure mix deserves inspection | Review the actual opening; choose one truthful opening change for the next draft or comparable upload |
| Opening is acceptable but a later curve segment falls | A scene, transition, redundancy or audience expectation may be involved | Inspect the actual segment and nearby footage; state a scene-level hypothesis before naming a cut |
| Only average view duration is available | Average viewing can be compared, but no exact exit timestamp is known | Obtain the curve or review the draft; do not cut at the mean AVD timestamp |
| Metrics look strong but reach is limited | Topic demand, audience fit, exposure mix or other factors may matter | Examine comparable uploads and audience evidence; test one idea or format hypothesis |
| Views rise but the desired action does not | Reach and goal conversion may diverge | Check whether the intended audience and truthful request match the goal; measure that action separately |
| Results vary across similar attempts | Several causes remain possible | Retain the run of results, record changed factors and choose a focused next test |
| Window, denominator or baseline is incompatible | The apparent difference may be a comparison problem | Mark the conclusion inconclusive and name the needed data/checkpoint |

Swipe behavior at entry and a mid-video retention change describe different events. A late scene cannot be identified as the cause of an initial swipe from an aggregate ratio. AVD is a mean: everyone watching 15 seconds and half watching 5 seconds/half 25 seconds both average 15, with different viewing patterns.

Values above 100% for average percentage viewed can reflect repeated viewing; they do not mean every viewer completed the video. The source’s 81.1%, 85%, 32-second and 8–12-day suggestions remain attributed heuristics. Use the relevant definition, the creator’s comparison and the stated goal instead of treating them as automatic verdicts.

## Editing Or Deleting A Published Video

Preserve the original asset and pre-change observations. Verify the actual scene and problem before changing a published video. Prefer testing an alternative in a new draft or future upload when that answers the question.

YouTube’s editor documentation says saved changes cannot be reverted through the former restore-original feature. A live trim also changes the content version being measured. [YouTube trimming guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9057455?hl=en).

Source claims that deletion always harms a channel are unverified. Do not delete automatically because one performance observation looks weak. Identify the actual reason, including any independent need to remove content, and assess that decision on its own evidence.

## Keep An Experiment Record

Use the existing tracking spreadsheet’s actual structure when its connection is configured. This logical record can be mapped to its fields; it is not a request to create a replacement tracker.

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Identity | Experiment ID, video ID, source URL, channel and content version |
| Timing | Published time, observed time, age, timezone and exact measurement window |
| Goal | The outcome being optimized and why |
| Baseline | Actual comparison records and reasons they are eligible |
| Observation | Raw values, definitions, available counts and missing fields |
| Interpretation | Calculation, observed difference and uncertainty |
| Hypothesis | Candidate explanation and competing explanations |
| Action | One proposed change, kept separate from what was actually done |
| Checkpoint | Review time, measure and what would support or weaken the hypothesis |
| Evidence | Research card IDs, source URLs and timestamps |
| Outcome | Later observation, actual changes, conclusion and next decision |

A one-change comparison is easier to interpret, but topic, audience and distribution can still change. It is not automatically a randomized causal experiment. If raw counts for a defined rate are unavailable, do not manufacture them from a displayed percentage or substitute views for an unknown denominator.
