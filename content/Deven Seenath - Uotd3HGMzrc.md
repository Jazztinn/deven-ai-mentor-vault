Two Swedish creators describe a ranking-channel partnership, complementary production roles, competitor study, adaptation rather than direct copying, community policy discussion, and accountability. They report uploading the same material multiple times as making production easier and helping revenue, without details on what was reused or how often. The host presents revenue growth and promotes a creator program; interviewees report 9K total in one month and earlier 3K over two weeks, while host says cumulative channel results reached almost 16K–17K. Host clarifies creators already had experience and monetization before joining, so the testimony does not establish beginner results or program causation. ## Actions From This Record

- **Researcher inference:** For a creator partnership, agree on shared goals and ownership or revenue arrangement, then divide recurring work by complementary strengths such as scripting, editing, clip research, and editor outreach. (Findings 2, 6, 7.)
- **Source instruction:** When studying competitors, select elements that seem effective, adapt them to channel style, and omit elements that do not matter; avoid copying videos wholesale. (Findings 10.)
- **Researcher inference:** Treat revenue, RPM, and program-impact figures in this interview as attributed claims; verify currency, timeframe, metric definition, and channel records before using them as benchmarks. (Findings 1, 3, 4, 5.)
- **Researcher inference:** Use peer discussion and accountability check-ins as possible sources of context and motivation, while checking platform policy from an authoritative source before acting. (Findings 8.)
- **Source instruction:** Study competitor channels for ideas, then adapt only relevant elements to the creator’s own audience and style. (Findings 14.)
- **Researcher inference:** Before interpreting a creator-program growth story as beginner evidence, check what experience, monetization status, and revenue the creator already had before joining. (Findings 16, 17.)
