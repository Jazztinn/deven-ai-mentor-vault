## Summary

The speaker opens by reporting that CS Global Music had recently been terminated because of alleged use of stolen copyrighted music or clips, then says music royalties still work and promotes an alternative provider whose name captions render inconsistently as Duna, Luna, Looney, or Lun. He claims a 70% creator share versus about 50% at Blastoff, illustrates the difference with a $1,000 revenue example, claims higher RPMs, discloses a 5% referral commission from the provider’s share, and cites a channel with 52 million views and $1,883 post-share revenue in one month. He estimates that 30 million monthly views could produce $1,000 and 10 million could produce $200–$300, even without monetization. His described workflow is to upload via the YouTube app, select Twilight, use an ambience segment around 1:40, and set its volume to 12%; he also recommends videos at least 30 seconds long. He warns against stolen/copyrighted content, urges original and highly reformative edits, and says AI or AI captions are fine. He gives inconsistent and uncertain payout estimates and says payouts arrive two to three months later. He closes by suggesting larger creators negotiate while smaller creators use the 70% share. All provider, revenue, RPM, platform, eligibility, and policy statements are the speaker’s claims; captions alone do not verify them.
