## Principles

### Choose breadth to fit goal, then use framing to reach beyond core fans

Sources favoring broad reach tie it to audience ceiling or a goal of maximizing views. Other advice targets an ideal viewer persona by language, age, and interests; a participant says a narrow sub-niche can still gain views when execution works. These views can coexist: select topic scope for the goal and creator fit, then test whether the same idea can be framed for a wider audience. Broad framing may extend reach without abandoning a narrow subject.

**When It Applies:** Broad-reach guidance is conditional on a reach-maximizing goal. Persona-first framing matters when language, age, or interests shape comprehension. Narrow-niche success is anecdotal and depends on execution; no source establishes a universal trade-off.

**Researcher Application:** For one candidate, write its intended viewer, then draft one specialist framing and one broader framing of the same concept. Compare which is clearer and more appealing to those viewers before changing niche.

**Limits:** Examples and advice are creator claims; no independent audience or outcome data.

### Separate trend discovery from durable niche validation

Sources recommend cross-platform outlier scans and recent dashboard results to surface candidates. Other advice asks whether the idea has enough recurring stories, can support many distinct ideas, and can be reproduced. A 100-idea test is one speaker’s screening heuristic, not a validated threshold. A trend spike is a lead to investigate, while repeatability and production capacity are separate checks.

**When It Applies:** Cross-platform scans and daily views are short-window discovery signals. A 100-idea list and team reproduction test apply before committing resources; news and trend-dependent concepts may fade.

**Researcher Application:** Use a trend scan to collect candidates; check platform uptake, write distinct follow-up ideas, list recurring events, and test whether the format can be produced repeatedly. Keep any one-day or outlier spike separate from evidence of sustained interest.

**Limits:** Tool displays, platform predictions, and claimed multipliers or earnings remain unverified; repeated clips from one creator are not independent evidence.

### Treat saturation and market-size estimates as competing hypotheses

Some speakers interpret saturation as demand and recommend competing with stronger ideas or angles. Others describe a finite viewer pool divided across channels. Suggested market proxies vary: one leading channel’s recent 28-day views, versus several competitors’ six-month average views and outlier peaks. These proxies do not measure total addressable audience directly. Competitor declines may signal a weakening format, but can also reflect channel execution.

**When It Applies:** Competitor evidence is useful only with comparable channels, time windows, and formats. Neither saturation nor one channel’s recent performance settles whether an opportunity exists.

**Researcher Application:** Compare several similar channels across a longer window, separate typical views from outliers, and note whether declines affect peers too. Then test a distinct format or angle before concluding the topic itself is exhausted.

**Limits:** All market and platform claims are attributed; sample selection, analytics, and causal explanations are unavailable.

### Adapt a mechanism into an original concept

Sources call this replicate-and-reiterate or format bending: identify what makes a format legible, then apply it to a different subject or combine formats. Examples include ranking/commentary and a GTA-5-to-GTA-6 idea transfer. Predictions about a game’s release, future demand or earnings remain source-era claims.

**When It Applies:** The new subject must support the format and actual source material. A borrowed concept does not establish permission to reuse a video.

**Researcher Application:** Write the reusable mechanism, what the new subject changes and the original contribution. Create an actual draft rather than assuming a repost will reproduce the outcome.

**Limits:** The source sometimes mixes adaptation with re-upload advice; neither rights nor a causal performance gain is established.

### Inspect the audience and the actual video before diagnosing a niche

Sources recommend examining channels watched by the audience and distinguish weak niche demand from weak videos. Neither one low-view upload nor an audience’s country establishes a broken niche. The content, language, interests, exposure mix and comparable period must be considered.

**When It Applies:** Available audience reports may be aggregated or incomplete; actual viewers can have several interests. Country alone is not evidence of irrelevance.

**Researcher Application:** Compare the intended viewer with actual audience/content patterns, then inspect the video and an eligible baseline before choosing a niche pivot.

**Limits:** The source’s “cooked” verdict and rhetorical 100x idea multiplier are not validated diagnostics.

### Use past examples to propose the next test, without treating them as a forecast

Advice suggests studying competitor outliers and revisiting formats that previously worked on one’s own channel when a borrowed approach fails. The idea’s framing and packaging can matter alongside aggregate metrics. A source-selected outlier cannot tell us how often the method failed.

**When It Applies:** Choose comparable examples, preserve failures and note changes in topic, audience, window and format.

**Researcher Application:** Select one candidate mechanism or angle, state why it fits, and test it in a future comparable upload with a checkpoint.

**Limits:** The experiment ratios in the corpus conflict; no upload mix, multiplier or guaranteed return is established.

### AI and trend tools assist discovery without validating demand

Sources describe dashboard scans and team/manual discovery, with one speaker saying AI works better inside an established niche than when judging a new one. These are tool and operating opinions, not a demonstrated intelligence limit or current product comparison.

**When It Applies:** Tool data freshness, platform, metric definition, sample selection and missing visuals affect interpretation.

**Researcher Application:** Record the candidate and the actual examples behind a tool result, check the target platform and use human review of a draft before committing resources.

**Limits:** No proprietary score, AI summary or market-volume display proves demand or gives permission to use source assets.
