## Anti-hook starts with event or action

Card: hooks-B001-K002 · Kind: source advice.

Several speakers call an opening that skips a teaser and starts with the clip or event an anti-hook. Examples include a direct event statement and a visual action with little setup. One speaker says this works best when the scene itself can orient viewers; another frames it as leaving context to unfold during the video. One source describes the potential context gap as making viewers feel bad for leaving; that pressure tactic is a source claim, not an endorsed benefit.

**Conditions:** Consider for commentary, stories, or clips with a clear and compelling event. If viewers cannot infer what they are seeing, add enough context to orient them. The corpus presents both opening styles.

**Application (researcher inference):** Review the actual opening scene before deciding: if it reads on its own, test a direct entry; if it needs explanation, add a short, accurate premise.

**Limits:** Hook labels vary by speaker; some use anti-hook for action without context, while another calls a concise event line an anti-hook. Performance and audience-psychology claims are attributed and unverified. The referenced clips and their visual clarity were not inspected.
